# Writer's Note
- There's a species of very distinctly plumed kiwi-like nestless flightless bird which lay one-to-two highly conical eggs at a time and scoop them up onto their backs where they carry them.
- These birds possibly come from a predator-free ecosystem? Or otherwise one where they have very few natural predators? Perhaps their feathers are poisonous, and so they don't naturally have to worry about predators (I like this option)?
  - I like the idea that they carry their chicks on their backs too. The chicks likely haven't developed the poison in their feathers, and so may be appetizing to prey. So, it's safer to carry them on your back where if prey gets ahold of them they may also likely get ahold of you too, thus poisoning them.
- These birds are very friendly (no natural predator) and thus easily get along with sophonts (humans, possibly elves). The males might even do little jigs and grooves to music. Humans don't really have trouble eating them (but it is a hassle to prepare).
  - The feathers and skin contain upwards of 90% of the toxins, and so plucking and skinning gets you most of the way. Similar to how natives may eat Pitohui the meat is then coated in charcoal and cooked, extracting a majority of the remaining toxins and making it edible (just edible, not good or in the least bit appetizing, in fact I'd imagine leftover toxins probably still make you partially sick).
- Over time the birds are domesticated by people, mostly used for their eggs and ornamental purposes. They become a large part of ritual culture, being used during large celebrations and festivals as religious symbols. As they are domesticated their ability to balance things on their back is selected for and they may even be trained to balance on top of each other , forming bird stacks!
  - I'd imagine a big part of the end of these festivals is perhaps eating the birds?
